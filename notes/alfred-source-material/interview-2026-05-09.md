## The full interview answer (lightly cleaned for readability, otherwise verbatim)

### Origin

I just love the Alfred character from the Batman series with Michael Caine
as the actor. It was my favorite Alfred, the English butler.

I ran it two to three months ago, and I actually bought a Mac Mini M4 to
run it. It's been running ever since. It's really good.

### Channels and the inbox flow

I set it up on Telegram. I set it up on Discord, and Telegram acts as an
inbox for me. Anytime I find an article or a TikTok video or YouTube
interviews or a podcast that I feel I want Alfred to summarize and store in
the information and extract the learning, I will send it on Telegram.
After that, Alfred is going to process it and save the learning into my
Obsidian Vault. That Obsidian Vault is going to be synced across my
devices via iCloud, and that works really well.

### Use case: health champion

For me personally, Alfred acts as a health champion, so it reminds me that
I need to do my fluticasone nasal spray every morning. It logs it and
stores it in an Obsidian vault in a markdown document. After that, Alfred
also helps to track my exercise and workout.

### Use case: open-ended workout tracker (favorite)

One of the most annoying things about tracking workouts is tracking those
miscellaneous workouts that I do throughout the day. I don't usually have
one session where I sit there for a couple of hours to do a workout. I
usually do a few workouts here and there. If I work out, for example,
I'll do a set of bench presses, then after I did a bench press I'll go
back to work, and then after that I'll come back to do, let's say,
lateral raises. Tracking this in a normal way of tracking is really
troublesome, because I have to keep the app and session open and keep
the weights and all that stuff. It's not great.

What Alfred does for me is that it helps me to make this into an open
text entry. I can just tell Alfred in a health channel that, for
example, "40 lb kettlebell swing 60 reps," and Alfred is then going to
store it into a new row in the markdown file for the workout logs. It's
going to store whatever I say that I enter in plain language. After I'm
done with that, I just send it another text next time, for example, "30
Cossack squat with the same kettlebell," and it knows the kettlebell
weight already, so it will just do it. Next time I can just say "60 more
swings," so it's really good in that sense that it can just track what
I'm doing. This is really one of my favorite use case, an open-ended
activity tracker.

### Use case: medications/vitamins reminders

In addition to that, I also use it to track any medications or vitamins
that I need to track, like my daily creatine and fluticasone nasal spray
and just a bunch of things. It can remind me as well, so I can easily
say, "Hey, you need to remind me to bring my fluticasone nasal spray to
work at 8 a.m. on every Wednesday and Thursday." Just do that. You don't
have to set anything. Everything is just plain language, and it works
really well, surprisingly well.

### Use case: natural-language interface for the *arr stack

Of course, I have another use case that works really well as well, which
is to be the natural language interface for my usenet arr stack. I have
sonar radar and all the other stuff set up, so I can just say, "I want
to watch the boys." You will then just look it up, download it, set it
up ready for Plex, and then I can just go to my Apple TV and watch it.
You can just help me to manage all my stuff pretty well.

### Use case: insight surfacing from learnings

Another big one that I use is for surfacing the insights from my
learnings. I have a learning channel in which I can paste those links
that I mentioned to you before. There is an insight channel that several
times a day will just wake up and then send me a text message saying,
"Hey, I'm surfacing this interview with so-and-so, and this is a
learning that we got from it."

### Use case: the household, and replacing ChatGPT

So right now I use Alfred probably as much as my wife uses it. My wife
uses it for general management of the household, scheduling of the
kids, and asking a bunch of questions that we don't really use ChatGPT
anymore. It works really well. Of course, we use it to drive replies to
emails and stuff like that too. It's really good.

### Use case: Grace's 9th birthday thread (the killer story)

There's another thing that Alfred did for my daughter's birthday. When
last month we were talking about preparing for her birthday, I said,
"Why don't you just talk to Alfred about it?" I created a thread in
Discord and just told Alfred, "Hey, this is a go. Help my daughter to
prepare for her birthday." Then Alfred went and helped talk to my
daughter about how to prepare for the birthday.

Yeah, that was amazing.

### Use case: finance (long-term, not day trading)

Of course, I also use Alfred for finances, as well as for researching
stocks, following the market update for the Iran war, and then
recommending to me which stocks to buy. Of course, I don't use it for
day trading. I am not a day trading person, but it's more of a value
investing and long-term horizon kind of thing. Which one is a good buy
for the long term, because I definitely do not have the mental capacity
to follow up with it? Yeah, it's really cool.

### Why-I-built-him: Claude Code nuance

Vic's clarification:

> Before OpenClaw, there was no product that could make it work because
> they don't work directly with my Obsidian notes or even Notion. I can
> tell Claude or ChatGPT to do it, but then it's not the same because it
> doesn't really connect to my Obsidian notes or Notion. I think the key
> here is just something that can easily be programmed to do scheduling
> reminders and connect to other integrations very easily, and it just
> works.

> Later on Claude Code can do it because I can open it up on a specific
> folder, but I have to use the cloud app and go to the Code. I can't use
> it anywhere else, so OpenClaw is like the first project that could do
> something like this.

Note: codex's Gate-2 review (2026-05-09) flagged that the current Claude
Code product line has moved closer (channels, scheduled tasks, multiple
surfaces). Vic's framing is from when he first chose OpenClaw three
months ago. Codex's suggested rewrite for the post:

> "Claude Code has moved closer since then. It can read and write inside
> a working folder, run from the terminal or desktop app, and now has
> channels and scheduled tasks. But that still wasn't the shape I wanted.
> I wanted a house assistant: always running on my Mac Mini, already
> listening on Telegram and Discord, trusted with my Obsidian Vault, and
> available to my wife and kids without opening a coding tool."

Held for Vic's decision (factual review of the URL claims codex made,
