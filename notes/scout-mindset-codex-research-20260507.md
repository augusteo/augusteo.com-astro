**Structural Findings**

1. **Rows 1-5: Galef sourcing is structurally broken.** Rows 1-4 admit secondary-outline verification, not primary verification. That fails the contract. A book-authored claim can be primary, but the matrix needs edition/page/ebook locator or a directly accessible first-party excerpt. Row 4 is worse: I found no primary support for the quoted "actual behavioral change..." line. Treat it as unverifiable, possibly hallucinated.

2. **Row 3: the "four self-tests" claim is wrong.** Secondary outlines consistently list **five** tests: double standard, outsider, conformity, selective skeptic, status quo bias. The matrix omits the double-standard test and calls the set Chapter 6, while multiple outlines place it in Chapter 5. If the post teaches four tests, that must be framed as an editorial selection, not Galef's set.

3. **Spec promise "four asymmetries" is not supported as written.** Lord/Ross/Lepper, Ditto/Lopez, and Kahan support asymmetry-style mechanisms. Mercier/Sperber and Trivers support social/evolutionary accounts of reasoning and self-deception, not a fourth empirical "asymmetry" in the same sense. The promised four mechanisms are a category error.

4. **Rows 16 and Throughline: AOMT is overstated and mis-sourced.** The "Key predictors..." quote is not from Mellers et al. 2015 *Perspectives on Psychological Science*; it is from Mellers et al. 2015 *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied*. Also, the notes say AOMT was the strongest dispositional predictor. The accessible Mellers 2015 text points to fluid intelligence and political knowledge as stronger than active open-mindedness. The throughline should not say AOMT was the strongest predictor.

5. **Row 18: the central GJP numbers are not matrix-supported.** The matrix admits the 25-30% and 15-30% wording was not found in the paper body. Worse, "CIA analysts" is not supported by the visible public wording; the common claim is about an internal Intelligence Community prediction market, not cleanly "CIA analysts." This is load-bearing and currently fails.

6. **Row 19 creates an unresolved contradiction with the throughline.** Hauenstein 2025 does not merely "threaten Mellers 2014's training/teaming causal claim." It says method-variance controls substantially eliminated, reduced, or reversed some teaming/training effects and made latent superforecaster factors hard to identify. The post cannot keep "AOMT + updating + team aggregation beats CIA analysts by 25-30%" as a clean recipe while treating Hauenstein as a footnote.

7. **Row 21 is numerically false in the corrected source.** Sellier/Scopelliti/Morewedge 2019 was corrected from **29%** to **19%**; SAGE's corrigendum says the original percentage was miscalculated. Row 21 must be changed.

8. **Row 20 overstates the debiasing result and is stale for a current trainability claim.** Morewedge 2015 supports single-intervention debiasing effects, but the abstract distinguishes games and videos; it does not support the matrix's blanket "30%+ immediately" wording for the whole intervention class. "One-hour" also needs direct support. This is an applied efficacy claim, not a field-canonical mechanism citation, so it is stale under the stated rules.

9. **Row 11 omits direct replication pressure.** The Kahan motivated-numeracy claim is not clean. Persson et al. 2021 is a preregistered replication that reports no good evidence for motivated numeracy and says the cumulative evidence is weaker than previously thought. Connor et al. 2024 reports a modified replication but not increased polarization among high-numeracy participants. The matrix's silence is structural because the post explicitly promises the reader will learn why more numerate people can be less accurate on politicized topics.

10. **Rows 12-14 are unverifiable as matrix quotes.** Trivers, Mercier/Sperber, and Baron are book claims without page-level verification in the provided matrix. They may be real, but they are not audit-passing rows until the primary text location is supplied.

11. **Row 15 needs convention cleanup.** Brier 1950 is foundational, but the matrix's binary `(p-o)^2` explanation does not match how Mellers 2015 describes tournament Brier scores, which can range from 0 to 2 for two-outcome forecasts. If the post uses Brier as the recurring metric, this needs an explicit convention note.

12. **Rows 6-7 are basically sound, but the Gutenberg ID looks wrong.** The Darwin passage verifies at Project Gutenberg, but the supplied note says eText `#2087`; the visible Gutenberg page I found is `#2010`. Cosmetic, not structural.
